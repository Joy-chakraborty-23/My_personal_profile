RESEARCH STATEMENT · OCTOBER 2026

Reliable estimation and safe intelligence
for battery systems.

A research summary drawn from the October 2026 scientific project report. Distinctions between evidence, ongoing work and future questions are intentional.

Download PDF summary ↓Supporting case study →

Research motivation

I study intelligent lithium-ion battery management in settings where important internal states cannot be observed directly. Models can support estimation, but they are never perfect. Cloud-based digital twins offer extra computation, yet continuous data exchange can increase communication cost and expose control architectures to delays and connectivity problems.

My central question is whether cloud assistance can be requested selectively while leaving immediate safety and state estimation under embedded control.

Current research contribution

For my MS by Research at IIT Guwahati, I have developed a safety-gated, event-triggered edge–cloud digital-twin architecture for a four-cell lithium-ion battery system. The local controller combines an EKF-type observer, residual and uncertainty monitoring, operating margins, cell balancing, current derating and contactor logic. Risk indicators decide when additional cloud computation may be useful. Returned corrections are checked for consistency, confidence, freshness and basic physical feasibility before use.

The reported tested PIL configuration achieved approximately 82.8% fewer edge-to-cloud communications than its continuous-communication baseline. This result is configuration-specific, not an error bound or a general stability guarantee. My source report also documents relevant voltage, current, temperature and reference-SOC figures under HPPC and UDDS testing at 25°C; those figures are not, by themselves, estimator-accuracy comparisons.

How I work

I use reduced-order electrical and thermal models to retain physical interpretability and manageable embedded cost. I distinguish calibration conditions from dynamic validation, and prefer to test estimation and communication under fault scenarios rather than rely only on an idealized plant. The current workflow uses MATLAB/Simulink and embedded-oriented development; physical OPAL-RT and TI C2000 closed-loop CHIL validation is the next stage.

Research limitations

The trigger and safety-gate rules still rely partly on engineering thresholds rather than formally derived requirements. Interaction between detailed physics-informed models and the local estimator requires more systematic validation. Physical CHIL timing, broader cross-chemistry behavior and long-term SOH/RUL integration are not yet established in the fast safety-critical loop.

Proposed PhD research direction

I want to study estimator uncertainty and triggering conditions that admit defensible performance bounds; contractual safety checks for interactions between digital-twin components; and physically informed learning that can remain useful across batteries, temperatures and ageing states. An important part of that direction is connecting theoretical arguments to embedded measurement, including latency, quantisation, sensor faults, communication loss and recovery.
