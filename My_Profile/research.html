RESEARCH / CURRENT FOCUS

Trustworthy intelligence
for battery systems.

My central question: how can a battery manager benefit from higher-fidelity computation without making safety dependent on the cloud?

The research problem

State of charge, internal resistance and other important battery variables are estimated rather than measured directly. Cloud digital twins can help with higher-fidelity models, but persistent communication introduces delay, resource cost and network dependence.

I am developing an approach where the embedded controller continues estimating and enforcing safety independently. It requests extra cloud computation only when conditions suggest the added information may be valuable, and every returned correction is checked before use.

Three design principles

  1. Safety stays local. The edge estimator and safety supervisor remain responsible during outages.
  2. Communication is event-driven. Residuals, uncertainty and risk indicators guide requests.
  3. Cloud information is advisory. Stale, inconsistent or unsafe corrections are rejected or attenuated.

METHOD / SYSTEMS THINKING

From the state estimator to the safety gate.

01

Model the pack

Reduced-order electrical and thermal cell models; separate cell states for SOC, polarization and temperature.

02

Estimate locally

An EKF-type estimator uses measured and predicted battery signals, with residual and uncertainty monitoring.

03

Ask when useful

An event trigger evaluates model mismatch, confidence, operating stress and periodic synchronization.

04

Validate cloud feedback

A safety gate checks SOC range, voltage consistency, temperature, confidence and freshness of each correction.

RESEARCH STATEMENT

What I have tested, and what I want to understand next.

I am interested in battery-management architectures where physical models, online estimation and intelligent computation cooperate without obscuring the responsibilities of the embedded safety controller. My present work is an engineering foundation, not a completed theoretical proof.

The next scientific challenges are to connect event triggers to uncertainty and error bounds, verify the acceptance of cloud corrections, and measure real-time behaviour in physical controller hardware.
